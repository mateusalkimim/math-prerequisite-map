Linear algebra speaks of what is preserved — sum and scale, exactly, throughout the domain; calculus, of what happens near a point. In the order of study, neither requires the other, and that is why the series is a tree, and not a line.

Independent of order, not of nature: the derivative is itself a linear operator, and differentiating is finding the best linear approximation near the point. In multiple variables, the derivative becomes a matrix and the two merge — and that is where the geometry of the image resides.

And analytic geometry is within linear algebra — it is the region of it where there is a dot product, which gives distance and angle, and therefore shape.
In any dimension: the limit of three is for illustration, which needs to fit on paper, never the structure.
